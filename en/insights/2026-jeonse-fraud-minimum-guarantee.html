South Korea’s Jeonse Fraud Minimum-Guarantee Scheme — Minimum Support and Advance Payments from 13 November 2026

From 13 November 2026, eligible victims of jeonse fraud may apply for minimum support where recoveries and statutory assistance remain below one-third of the lease deposit. This report distinguishes minimum-support payments, advance payments and public-rental options.

Key takeaways

  • The scheme does not add one-third of the deposit to every victim’s recovery. Distributions, repayments and statutory support are deducted before the shortfall is calculated.[1][2]
  • Eligibility is limited to natural persons meeting the statutory victim, nationality and other requirements. A victim determination and a payment determination are separate stages.[1]
  • Licensed-real-estate-agent compensation that substantively restores the deposit is included among the statutory deductions.[2]
  • Failure to demand a distribution or allocation can restrict eligibility. A specific transitional rule governs when this restriction begins to apply.[1]
  • Minimum support, advance payment and public-rental assistance should be compared before payment. Statutory public-rental support is unavailable after the cash payment is received.[1][4]
  • The special Act is temporary and currently expires on 31 May 2027. It applies to first lease agreements concluded on or before 31 May 2025.[6]

01. The Minimum-Guarantee Scheme Takes Effect on 13 November 2026

Act No. 21634, promulgated on 12 May 2026, introduces minimum deposit support and an advance-payment-and-settlement mechanism. The Enforcement Decree was amended on 13 October 2026, and the Enforcement Rule establishes application, determination, objection and repayment procedures. The provisions governing minimum-support and advance payments take effect on 13 November 2026.[1][2][3]

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport announced that online applications through LH Apply would open on 10 November, while in-person applications at LH regional offices would open on 13 November. Early availability of an online filing channel does not itself establish statutory eligibility or entitlement to payment.[7]

The Act Is Temporary, Not Permanent

The Jeonse Fraud Victim Support Act was originally enacted as a temporary law effective for two years. A 2025 amendment extended its effective period to four years, and its current expiry date is 31 May 2027. The 2025 amendment also limits the Act to tenants whose first lease agreement was concluded on or before 31 May 2025.[6]

A person who was determined to be a qualifying victim, or who filed an application for such determination before expiry, may continue to be governed by the Act after that date. Certain bodies and systems required to complete determinations and support work may also continue under transitional provisions.[6]

An Existing Victim Determination Does Not Trigger Automatic Payment

A person determined to be a victim before commencement must still apply separately for a minimum-support or advance payment and receive a calculation and payment determination. Victim status and a payment determination are separate stages.

02. Confirm That the Applicant Is a Natural Person, the Relevant Victim Type and a Korean National

Article 3(1) limits an application for determination as a jeonse-fraud victim to a natural person. A company or other organisation cannot obtain a victim determination under this provision.[1]

The minimum-support payment is available to qualifying jeonse-fraud victims and certain tenants under Article 2, item 4(c), who fall within the statutory category of a minimum-guarantee victim. Article 25-9 excludes a person who does not hold nationality of the Republic of Korea from that category.[1]

03. Minimum Support Is One-Third of the Deposit Less Amounts Already Recovered

Article 4-4 of the Enforcement Decree establishes the following calculation.[2]

**Minimum-support payment = one-third of the lease deposit

− distributions received through auction or public sale

− amounts repaid through the deposit-return claim

− statutory support, including auction surplus and rent support**

Amounts Deducted

DeductionPrincipal amounts covered by the legislation
Amounts obtained by exercising opposability or preferential-payment rightsDistributions received through auction or public sale
Repayment through the lease-deposit return claimAmounts recovered from the landlord or from a purchaser of a dwelling subject to an opposable lease
Substantive recovery of the depositAmounts that substantively restore the lease deposit through compensation under Article 30 of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act or similar means
Existing support under the special ActAuction surplus, specified rent support and other statutory support

Scroll horizontally to read the full table.

Compensation paid under the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act may differ in legal character from the tenant’s deposit-return claim against the landlord. Nevertheless, the Enforcement Decree expressly includes compensation under Article 30 of that Act and similar amounts where they substantively restore the deposit. The payment’s stated basis and its connection with actual deposit recovery must therefore be examined.[2]

Illustration

For a deposit of KRW 150 million, the minimum-guarantee amount is KRW 50 million. If the tenant has received KRW 20 million through an auction distribution and KRW 10 million as auction-surplus support, the aggregate recovery is KRW 30 million and the minimum-support calculation begins with the remaining KRW 20 million.

This is an illustration of the structure only. The actual calculation must identify every statutory deduction, including qualifying compensation from a licensed real-estate agent, recoveries from a landlord or purchaser, and rent support already paid.

One-Third of the Deposit Is Not a Cap on the Underlying Claim

The scheme supplements a minimum level of recovery. It does not reduce the balance of the tenant’s deposit-return or damages claims against the landlord or other liable persons to one-third of the deposit.

If additional deposit amounts are recovered after support is paid, however, notification and repayment obligations may arise up to the relevant statutory limit.[3]

04. Failure to Demand a Distribution Can Restrict Eligibility

Article 25-9(2) excludes the following cases from the minimum-support payment.[1]

RestrictionMain rule
Direct purchase of the affected propertyThe victim purchased the jeonse-fraud or trust-fraud property personally
No demand for distribution or allocationThe victim failed to make the required demand and thereby failed to exercise the preferential-payment right
Receipt of an advance paymentThe victim received all or part of the minimum-guarantee amount in advance

Scroll horizontally to read the full table.

The restriction does not concern every case in which the distribution was small or the preferential-payment right was ultimately unsuccessful. The statutory language concerns failure to make a demand for distribution under the Civil Execution Act or an allocation request under national or local tax-collection legislation.

This restriction applies to proceedings in which the first auction-commencement decision or public-sale notice occurs on or after 13 November 2026. Where commencement or notice occurred earlier, the transitional provision must be examined separately.[1]

The Distribution Deadline May Arrive Before the Victim Determination

The victim-determination process and an auction can proceed on different timelines. Track the auction commencement and the distribution-demand deadline independently rather than waiting for the victim determination.

05. Compare Minimum Support with Public-Rental Support Before Payment

A person who receives a minimum-support payment cannot then receive public-rental support under Articles 25, 25-2 or 25-3. The same rule applies to an advance-payment recipient.[1][4]

The Act does not list prior receipt of some public-rental assistance as a stand-alone bar to applying for minimum support. Instead, auction surplus and rent support received before payment are deducted from the minimum-support calculation. A person already living in public rental housing or receiving rent support should therefore confirm with LH how previous support will be deducted and how housing support will end or transition after a minimum-support determination. This follows from reading the non-duplication and deduction provisions together.[1][2]

Public-rental assistance also differs by route.

  • Where a public-housing operator purchases and supplies the affected property, the victim may generally remain for up to ten years and, subject to the statutory conditions, may extend for another ten years or receive the remaining auction surplus.
  • Other preferential public-rental supply under Article 25-2 may also permit up to ten years of residence followed by a further period of up to ten years at the applicable rent level.[4]

The comparison should therefore cover long-term housing stability and the treatment of remaining surplus, not only the immediate cash amount.

The Relationship Between the Victim’s and the Public Operator’s Preferential Purchase Rights Has Also Changed

Under the 2026 amendment, a public-housing operator can exercise its preferential purchase right without providing the security otherwise required under civil-execution legislation.

Where both the victim and the public-housing operator have filed preferential-purchase declarations, the victim may request authorisation of the victim’s own purchase declaration by the sale date. If such a request is made, the court or public-sale authority must authorise the sale to the victim.[4]

Before applying, a victim should therefore compare personal purchase of the property, a purchase request to the public operator followed by public-rental occupancy, and minimum support. Personal purchase of the affected property restricts eligibility for the minimum-support payment.

06. Advance Payment Is a Pay-First, Settle-Later Mechanism for Specified Unauthorised-Lease Cases

The advance-payment scheme applies to specified minimum-guarantee victims, including certain tenants who contracted with a person lacking lawful authority to lease the property and certain victims of trust fraud. It pays all or part of the minimum-guarantee amount before disposal is completed.[1]

Advance-Payment Calculation

**Advance payment = one-third of the lease deposit

− amounts already repaid through the lease-deposit return claim

− statutory rent support received before the advance payment**

Because the final distribution or auction surplus is not yet fixed, the deduction structure differs from the minimum-support calculation.[2]

Assignment of the Claim and Maintenance of Opposability

Where the Minister or the delegated institution acquires the applicant’s deposit-return claim, it succeeds to the preferential-payment right to the extent of the acquired amount. An advance-payment recipient must maintain opposability under the Housing Lease Protection Act until settlement is completed.[1]

A proposed move, change of resident registration or loss of possession should therefore be reviewed in advance for its effect on opposability and potential repayment exposure.

Where the Settlement Amount Is Higher or Lower Than the Advance Payment

The Enforcement Decree provides the following.[2]

Settlement resultTreatment
Settlement amount exceeds the advance paymentThe excess is paid to the victim
Settlement amount is equal to or below the advance paymentNo additional payment is made

Scroll horizontally to read the full table.

The legislation does not require immediate repayment of the difference merely because the settlement amount is below the advance payment. If the victim later recovers additional deposit amounts, however, the recovered amount must be repaid up to the unsettled difference between the advance payment and the settlement amount.[2][3]

For example, if the advance payment is KRW 30 million and the settlement amount is KRW 10 million, the unsettled difference is KRW 20 million. A later recovery of KRW 5 million from the landlord would trigger repayment of KRW 5 million, while cumulative repayments cannot exceed KRW 20 million.

07. Manage the Application Stage, Filing Period and Objection Separately

LH administers payment, settlement and repayment of minimum-support and advance payments.[2]

When to Apply for Minimum Support

A minimum-support application is filed once the recovery amount can be calculated.

  • Where the victim requested purchase of the property by LH or another public-housing operator: after the purchase and calculation of auction surplus and related support are completed.
  • In other cases: after the auction or public-sale distribution, or disposal of a trust property, is completed.[3]

When to Apply for an Advance Payment

An advance-payment application must be filed before disposal is completed.

  • Where LH was asked to purchase a trust-fraud property: before the purchase is completed.
  • For other eligible applicants: before disposal of the affected property is completed.[3]

Filing Period

As a rule, an application must be filed within three years from the victim determination. If an auction or public sale or another unavoidable circumstance made filing within that period impossible, the application may be filed within one year from completion of the auction or public-sale process.[5]

For a person already determined to be a victim before commencement, the period begins on 13 November 2026. The three-year period is generally described as running until 13 November 2029, but the separate one-year rule following completion of an auction or public sale and the filing deadline stated in LH’s operational notice should also be checked.[5]

Payment Determination and Objection

LH must generally determine eligibility and amount within 90 days of the application. Where unavoidable, the period may be extended once by up to 30 days.[1]

Article 14 of the Enforcement Rule permits an objection regarding eligibility or amount to be filed with LH on the prescribed form under Article 36 of the General Act on Public Administration. The statutory period under that provision is 30 days from receipt of the disposition.[3]

The following dates should be tracked separately:

application → request for supplementation → supplementary filing → payment determination notice → objection deadline

08. Later Recovery, Personal Purchase or Loss of Opposability Can Trigger Repayment

After a minimum-support or advance payment, the following events can create a duty to report and repay or allow LH to demand repayment.[1][3]

OverpaymentThe amount overpaid

A repayment event must be reported to LH without delay. Failure to repay within the required period can lead to collection in the same manner as national taxes.[3]

Failure to report, making a false report, or failing to repay within the required period may be subject to an administrative fine of up to KRW 30 million.[1]

The Payment and the Right to Receive It Are Protected

A minimum-support or advance payment, and the right to receive it, cannot be assigned, pledged or seized. The protection is intended to preserve the payment for the victim’s recovery and housing stability.[1]

09. LAW NOAH Analysis — Organise Three Matters Before Applying

1) Prepare a Complete Schedule of Every Amount Actually Recovered

The calculation does not end by multiplying the deposit by one-third.

Record, by date, auction distributions, public-sale allocations, payments from the landlord or purchaser, amounts that substantively restore the deposit through licensed-real-estate-agent compensation, auction surplus and rent support.

DatePayer or institutionAmountStated basisStatutory deduction?Evidence

Scroll horizontally to read the full table.

Whether an amount is deductible is not an election by the applicant. It depends on whether the payment falls within the distributions, repayments or support listed in Article 4-4 of the Enforcement Decree. In particular, compensation from a licensed real-estate agent is deductible where it substantively restores the deposit.[2]

2) Compare Cash Support with Long-Term Housing Support Before the Payment Determination

Once minimum support or an advance payment is paid, the statutory public-rental support cannot subsequently be received.

Before the payment determination, compare:

  • whether long-term residence in the present dwelling or nearby area is required;
  • the practical value of up to ten years of rent support and any additional occupancy period;
  • whether personal purchase of the affected property is realistic;
  • whether rent support funded from auction surplus or an immediate minimum-support payment better meets the victim’s needs; and
  • how much public-rental rent support already received will be deducted from the minimum-support calculation.

The critical point is not merely submission of the application but the payment determination. The consequences of the available routes should be checked before payment eliminates later access to the public-rental route.

3) Put the Victim Determination, Auction, Distribution Demand and Support Application on One Timeline

These procedures can progress independently:

lease agreement → loss → application for victim determination → auction commencement → distribution-demand deadline → victim determination → sale and distribution → support application → determination → objection → later recovery and repayment

The distribution-demand deadline can pass before the victim determination is issued. Managing only the support application is therefore insufficient.

The minimum-support restriction for failing to demand a distribution or allocation applies to proceedings first commenced or publicly noticed on or after 13 November 2026. Even for earlier proceedings, however, the distribution demand can remain critical to actual recovery and the parties’ legal rights.[1]

The amendment does not transfer every loss caused by jeonse fraud to the State.

Its basic structure is to supplement a minimum level of recovery where distributions, repayments and statutory support combined remain below one-third of the deposit, subject to the statutory requirements and application process.

Before applying, check the victim-determination type, Korean nationality, actual recoveries, distribution demands, the relationship with housing assistance, the disposal stage and the applicable filing period.
