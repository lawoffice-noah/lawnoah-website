South Korea’s 2026 Equal Employment Act Amendments — Workplace Sexual Harassment Fines, Four Paid Days of Infertility-Treatment Leave and Childcare Reduced-Hours Notices

From 27 November 2026, the Act expressly expands direct administrative-fine exposure for workplace sexual harassment, increases paid infertility-treatment leave to the first four days, and specifies notice deadlines for childcare reduced working hours.

Key points

  • The principal amendments take effect on 27 November 2026.
  • A corporate representative is expressly added to the workplace sexual-harassment provisions.
  • Training coverage expressly includes employers, corporate representatives, supervisors and workers.
  • Direct administrative-fine exposure expands to corporate representatives and specified relatives of employers or representatives who work as supervisors or employees.
  • Infertility-treatment leave remains six days per year, with the first four days paid.
  • Adverse treatment because of infertility-treatment leave may attract up to three years’ imprisonment or a criminal fine up to KRW 30 million.
  • A timely childcare reduced-hours application generally requires approval and written or electronic notice within 14 days of the application.

01. What changes from 27 November 2026

Act No. 21700 was promulgated on 26 May 2026 and takes effect on 27 November 2026. Presidential Decree No. 36816 was promulgated on 10 November 2026 and takes effect on the same date.[1][2]

02. Corporate representatives were not entirely outside the previous regime

The prior Act referred to employers, supervisors or workers. The amendment expressly adds the representative of a corporation.[1] Even before the amendment, the Ministry of Government Legislation had interpreted a corporate representative director as falling within the persons subject to workplace sexual-harassment prevention training.[6]

The more important effect of the amendment is to remove interpretive uncertainty by naming corporate representatives directly in the definition, prohibition and administrative-fine provisions.

03. Training rules expressly identify representatives and supervisors

Amended Article 13(2) expressly identifies the employer, corporate representative, supervisor and worker as persons who must receive prevention training. Businesses should ensure that actual attendance records include representatives and employees with supervisory authority.

04. Direct fines for the harassing conduct expand

Before the amendment, the provision imposing an administrative fine for the harassing conduct itself referred directly to an employer. From 27 November 2026, it extends to the employer, corporate representative, and specified relatives of the employer or representative who work as supervisors or employees. The statutory ceiling is KRW 10 million.[1]

Ordinary supervisors and workers remain subject to the prohibition on harassment, but the amendment does not make every ordinary harasser directly subject to this particular fine. The employer’s investigation, victim-protection and disciplinary duties remain separately relevant.

05. Relatives include spouses and relatives within the fourth degree

The amended Decree identifies the employer’s or corporate representative’s spouse, blood relatives within the fourth degree and relatives by marriage within the fourth degree, where they work at the business as a supervisor or employee.[2]

06. Fine levels are KRW 2m, 3m, 5m and 10m

The Decree and the Ministry’s official explanatory material set out the following levels.[3]

ConductFine
Ordinary harassment by employer or corporate representativeKRW 3 million
Ordinary harassment by a covered relative working as supervisor or employeeKRW 2 million
Repeated harassment of one person or harassment of two or more personsKRW 5 million
Harassment again within three years after a prior harassment-fine dispositionKRW 10 million

The higher KRW 5 million and KRW 10 million levels are not limited to representatives; they may apply irrespective of the basic actor category where the stated aggravating conditions are met.

07. The harassment itself and retaliation carry different sanctions

Under this Act, the workplace sexual-harassment conduct itself is subject to an administrative fine, not imprisonment merely because it meets the Act’s harassment definition. Separate criminal offences such as indecent assault may of course apply where their elements are independently satisfied.

By contrast, adverse treatment of a harassment victim or reporter is criminally punishable under the Act, with potential imprisonment up to three years or a criminal fine up to KRW 30 million.[7]

08. Investigation, protection and disciplinary duties remain

Expanding direct fines does not replace the employer’s duties to investigate, protect the victim, take appropriate measures and avoid retaliation.

09. LAW NOAH Analysis — separate protocols for representative or owner-family cases

Where the alleged harasser is the representative or a member of the owner’s family, the ordinary reporting chain may not be sufficiently independent. Businesses should decide in advance who receives the complaint, who authorises the investigation, when an outside investigator is used, who orders protective measures, whether the board or auditor receives a report, and how the alleged harasser’s influence over appraisal, assignment and promotion is restricted.

10. Infertility-treatment leave remains six days, with four paid days

The annual entitlement remains six days. What changes is the paid portion: from the first two days to the first four days.[1]

Through 26 Nov 2026From 27 Nov 2026
Annual leave6 days6 days
PaidFirst 2 daysFirst 4 days
Remaining unpaid portionUp to 4 daysUp to 2 days

11. Existing users are covered, but do not receive a fresh four-day block

The supplementary provisions apply the new rule to workers who had already used or were using infertility-treatment leave when the amendment commenced. Days used before commencement count toward the first four days.[1]

If three days were used before commencement, the fourth day used after commencement falls within the paid first four days. If four or more days had already been used, later fifth or sixth days do not become paid merely because the amendment commenced. The amendment does not expressly require retroactive re-payment of earlier days that were lawfully unpaid under the former rule.

12. Retaliation for infertility-treatment leave becomes criminally punishable

From 27 November 2026, an employer that dismisses, disciplines or otherwise disadvantages a worker because the worker used infertility-treatment leave may face up to three years’ imprisonment or a criminal fine up to KRW 30 million.[1]

Performance appraisal, promotion, reassignment, contract renewal, bonuses, discipline and termination should therefore be supported by objective reasons independent of the leave use.

13. Employment-insurance support also expands to four days

The employer’s duty to provide paid leave and the employment-insurance benefit are separate. The employer’s statutory paid-leave duty applies regardless of business size, while the government benefit is directed to insured workers at priority-support enterprises.

Government guidance states that support expands to the first four days, with a maximum of KRW 336,840, subject to the worker’s ordinary wage and separate employment-insurance eligibility requirements.[5]

14. Infertility-treatment information requires tighter access controls

The Act prohibits disclosure, against the worker’s wishes, of information learned while processing an infertility-treatment leave request. HR teams should therefore separately control access to applications and medical proof, how much information is shared with supervisors, whether the specific reason appears in attendance systems, and whether leave use flows into appraisal records.

15. Childcare reduced hours — timely applications require action within 14 days

From 27 November 2026, where a worker applies under Article 15(1) of the Enforcement Decree, the employer must generally allow the reduced hours and notify the worker in writing or electronically within 14 days of the application.[2]

A timely application is generally filed at least 30 days before the intended start date. A late application follows a separate procedure under which the employer designates a commencement date within 30 days and permits the arrangement.

16. Exceptions to the duty to allow reduced hours remain

The Act generally requires reduced hours for a worker raising a child aged 12 or younger or in sixth grade or below, subject to statutory exceptions. Where a lawful exception applies, the employer may refuse, but must provide the reason in writing and discuss alternative support such as childcare leave or adjusted starting and finishing times.

17. Does missing the 14-day notice trigger a separate fine?

As of the review date, the amended fine provisions do not separately identify a case where the employer actually grants the reduced-hours arrangement but merely fails to issue the approval notice within 14 days as an independent fine item. By contrast, unjustified refusal of the reduced-hours arrangement itself may attract an administrative fine.

Businesses should therefore distinguish the substantive duty to allow / 14-day notice of allowance / written reason and consultation on refusal. The Supreme Court has also recognised an employer duty to accommodate the work-family balance of workers raising children.[8]

18. LAW NOAH Analysis — redesign the workflow, not only the policy

1) Workplace sexual-harassment cases

  • Include representatives and supervisors in training
  • Manage conflicts where representatives or owner-family members are involved
  • Set criteria for external investigators
  • Protect victims and prevent retaliation
  • Create an alternative reporting line for representative-related complaints

2) Infertility-treatment leave

  • Reflect six days / first four days paid
  • Calculate remaining paid days for existing users
  • Distinguish employment-insurance eligibility
  • Restrict access to infertility-related information
  • Prevent adverse HR treatment after leave use

3) Childcare reduced hours

  • Record the application date automatically
  • Distinguish timely from late applications
  • Review statutory exceptions
  • Manage 14-day or 30-day deadlines
  • Retain written/electronic notice evidence
  • Document reasons and alternative-support consultation on refusal

Work rules → application form → approval workflow → payroll → appraisal → evidence retention should operate as one connected process.

Conclusion

The amendment clarifies direct sanctions for harassment by corporate representatives and specified owner-family members, expands paid infertility-treatment leave while criminalising retaliation, and makes approval-notice timing for childcare reduced hours more concrete.

Businesses should move beyond policy wording and test the actual process: application or complaint → independent decision-maker → statutory deadline → notice → protection/payroll treatment → record retention.
