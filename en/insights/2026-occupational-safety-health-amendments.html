South Korea’s 2026 Occupational Safety and Health Act Amendments — Expanded Stop-Work Rights and Repeat-Fatality Surcharges

The 2026 amendments expand stop-work rights and requests, work restrictions before corrective measures are completed, repeat-fatality surcharges, weather-related construction extensions and occupational safety-cost duties in stages.

Key takeaways

  • Workers may stop work and evacuate where an imminent risk of industrial accident exists or is reasonably feared.
  • Workers, worker representatives and honorary occupational safety and health inspectors gain a statutory right to request stop-work and protective measures.
  • Workers of a related contractor may, in defined circumstances, request measures directly from the principal contractor.
  • Workers must not be returned to the affected work before the required safety and health measures are completed.
  • Where safety or health duties caused three or more worker deaths in one year, an administrative surcharge of up to 5% of operating profit may apply.
  • Heatwaves and cold waves are expressly added as construction-period extension grounds, while safety-management-cost duties expand across subcontracting and designated non-construction sectors.

01. The amendments commence in four stages

The amendment was promulgated as Act No. 22122 on 27 October 2026. The general commencement date is 28 April 2027, six months after promulgation, but several provisions have separate dates.[1]

CommencementMain changes
27 October 2026Heatwaves and cold waves expressly added as construction-period extension grounds; broader safety-certification exemption for imports intended for export
28 January 2027Safe Workplace Committee and whistleblower reward scheme
28 April 2027Expanded stop-work framework, work restrictions following corrective orders, broader ministerial suspension orders, repeat-fatality surcharges, registration-cancellation requests and stronger construction-sector safety-cost rules
28 October 2027Safety-management-cost duties for subcontracting in designated non-construction industries

A compliance plan should therefore not treat the reform as a single change “starting in 2027.” Each provision’s commencement and transitional rule requires separate review.

The restriction following a corrective order applies to corrective measures ordered after commencement; the broader ministerial suspension power applies to qualifying accidents occurring after commencement; and the registration-cancellation request provision applies where a new ground for a business-suspension request arises after commencement.[1]

02. Existing stop-work rights already protect decisions based on reasonable grounds

Current Article 52 allows a worker to stop work and evacuate where there is an imminent risk of an industrial accident. This does not mean the worker must first prove that the danger had objectively materialised.

The Supreme Court has held that it is sufficient for a worker to have reasonable grounds to believe that an imminent risk existed. Potential danger, the nature of the accident and the measures taken by relevant authorities must be assessed together, and the employer may not impose disadvantageous treatment merely because the worker stopped work on that basis.[4]

The amendment expressly adds situations where danger is feared

From 28 April 2027, Article 52(1) will apply where an imminent risk of industrial accident exists or is feared. The statutory wording therefore expressly reaches an earlier risk stage, while building on the protection already recognised in case law.[3]

It would be inaccurate to say that workers previously had to wait until an accident was immediately about to occur. The better reading is that the amendment makes the protected risk threshold more explicit and expands the text of the right itself.

Reporting and restrictions on resuming work

After stopping work and evacuating, the worker must promptly report to the supervisor or department head. The responsible manager must take necessary safety and health measures, and the amendment expressly prohibits assigning the worker to that work before the measures are complete.

Businesses should document a sequence of risk identification → stop-work and evacuation → reporting → site assessment → corrective measures → completion confirmation → approval and record of resumption.

03. Workers and their representatives may request stop-work measures

Amended Article 51 creates a statutory request mechanism. From 28 April 2027, a worker, worker representative or honorary occupational safety and health inspector may request stop-work, evacuation or other necessary measures where an imminent risk exists or is feared, including where a failure to perform statutory safety or health duties may create serious hazards.[3]

Upon receiving the request, the business owner must take the required measures unless there are special grounds not to do so.

The meaning of “special grounds” has not yet been settled

The amended Act does not define or list examples of special grounds. No implementing decree, rule or settled precedent has yet developed this new exception.

It would therefore be premature either to rule out production disruption or financial loss in all cases, or to treat any managerial judgment as sufficient. Existing case law on reasonable grounds for a worker’s own stop-work decision may be informative, but it is not identical to the new exception available to a business owner or contractor under Article 51.[4]

  • The risk identified in the request
  • The results of the site assessment
  • Objective grounds for concluding that no risk exists or that it has been removed by another measure
  • Alternative safety measures taken
  • The responsible decision-maker and time of decision
  • The explanation provided to the requesting party

04. Subcontractor workers may make a direct request to the principal contractor

Where workers of a related contractor perform work at the contractor’s workplace, those workers, their representative or an honorary inspector may request necessary measures directly from the principal contractor as well as from their own employer. The principal contractor must also act unless special grounds exist.[3]

A principal contractor can no longer rely solely on an instruction to “raise the issue with your own employer.” It needs a direct intake route, a joint assessment process, emergency authority, allocation of corrective responsibilities, resumption approval and records.

The new request route sits alongside existing criminal exposure

If a contractor breaches the safety and health duties in Article 63 and thereby causes the death of a related contractor’s worker, Article 167(1) may impose imprisonment for up to seven years or a criminal fine of up to KRW 100 million.[9]

The Supreme Court assesses whether a party is a statutory contractor or a construction project owner by substance rather than label, including whether it led and controlled construction, its practical power and influence, expertise and construction capability. Article 63 duties may also constitute the duty of care for occupational negligent homicide or injury.[10]

The new request right therefore adds an earlier intervention route; it does not replace the principal contractor’s existing duties.

05. No disadvantage may be imposed for a stop-work request, stoppage or evacuation

Amended Article 51(5) prohibits dismissal or other disadvantageous treatment of a worker or other protected person who requested stop-work measures with reasonable grounds to believe that an imminent risk existed or was feared. Article 52(4) separately protects a worker who personally stopped work and evacuated on the same basis.

RouteProtected person
Article 51Worker or other protected person who requested measures from the business owner or contractor
Article 52Worker who personally stopped work and evacuated

Where a business owner violates Article 52(4), the worker who personally stopped work and evacuated may seek relief before the Labor Relations Commission under Article 28 of the Labor Standards Act through new Article 52(5).[3]

Although disadvantageous treatment is also prohibited for Article 51 requesters, the express Labor Relations Commission route in Article 52(5) is textually directed to workers who stopped work and evacuated themselves.

06. A business owner’s own duty to stop work after a corrective order is introduced

Under the current structure, the Minister of Employment and Labor may order the suspension, replacement, removal or improvement of machinery or facilities, and may later issue a separate work-suspension order if the order is not implemented and the danger continues.

From 28 April 2027, amended Article 53(3) will require a business owner who has received a corrective order to stop assigning the affected work, without waiting for a further ministerial suspension order, where the work creates or is feared to create an imminent industrial-accident risk in circumstances to be prescribed by ministerial rule. The work must remain stopped until the corrective measure is completed.[3]

The obligation is contained in the Act itself, but the detailed qualifying circumstances remain to be specified in subordinate legislation.

  • The risk associated with the machinery or facility under the corrective order
  • Whether the future ministerial rule classifies the work as prohibited
  • The scope of the stoppage
  • Completion of the corrective measure
  • Approval and record of work resumption

07. The Minister’s work-suspension power is broadened

Amended Article 55 extends the power beyond serious accidents. It may apply where a worker is unconscious or missing after an accident, or suffers serious physical or physiological impairment as defined by presidential decree, and an imminent risk of recurrence exists at the workplace.[5]

The order may cover the same type of work as the accident. It may also cover workplace operations where collapse, fire, explosion or leakage creates an imminent risk of a serious accident, or where an accident may spread to surrounding areas.

The expanded provision applies to qualifying serious accidents or industrial accidents occurring on or after 28 April 2027.[1]

08. Repeat-fatality cases may attract an operating-profit-based administrative surcharge

New Article 161-2 allows an administrative surcharge where a breach of Articles 38, 39 or 63 caused industrial accidents resulting in three or more worker deaths within the most recent year.[6]

Structure of the statutory ceilings

SituationStatutory ceiling
General ruleUp to 5% of operating profit
No operating profit or operating profit cannot be calculatedUp to KRW 3 billion
Five per cent of operating profit is below KRW 3 billionUp to KRW 3 billion only in circumstances prescribed by presidential decree

The last category requires additional conditions prescribed by presidential decree. It should not be read as allowing KRW 3 billion whenever five per cent happens to be below that amount.

The Ministry’s official explanatory material discusses a three-year average operating-profit approach. The Act itself, however, delegates the detailed calculation and assessment rules to the presidential decree, so the final formula must be checked when the subordinate legislation is issued.[2]

Separate statutory considerations from matters left to subordinate legislation

The Act directly requires the rules to consider factors such as violation frequency, business size and worker deaths. In an individual decision, the Minister must consider the nature and seriousness of the violation, the ratio of deaths to the workforce, and the efforts made to prevent and remedy industrial accidents.

Those considerations are already statutory. The detailed formula, adjustments and the composition and operation of the surcharge review committee remain matters for subordinate legislation.

The surcharge applies to worker deaths caused by post-commencement breaches of safety or health duties. It should not be assumed that pre-commencement deaths automatically count toward the one-year threshold.[1]

09. Repeated business suspensions may lead to a request to cancel registration or a licence

Amended Article 159(2) permits the Minister to request the competent administrative authority to cancel or revoke the relevant business registration or licence where a business owner has received two or more business-suspension dispositions in the previous three years following Article 159(1) requests and again becomes subject to such a request.[6]

  • An accident prescribed by presidential decree caused by breaching Articles 38, 39 or 63, including cases involving many worker deaths or serious harm to the surrounding area
  • A fatal industrial accident caused by violating an order under Article 53(1) or (4)

A business-suspension disposition that is finally invalidated or cancelled by a court is excluded from the count.

The Ministry does not directly cancel the registration

This is a request by the Minister to the competent authority. Actual cancellation or revocation follows the sector-specific registration or licensing law—such as construction or electrical-construction legislation—and the competent authority’s procedure. The authority must generally comply absent a justifiable reason.

The treatment of pre-commencement suspensions should not be stated categorically

The supplementary provisions apply new Article 159(2) where a fresh ground for a business-suspension request arises on or after 28 April 2027. They do not expressly exclude earlier suspension dispositions from the three-year count.[1]

The Ministry’s explanatory release, however, describes the measure in terms of two or more suspensions during the three years from commencement. Until implementing rules or authoritative practice clarify the point, businesses should not assume either interpretation is settled.[2]

10. Heatwaves and cold waves are expressly recognised as construction-period extension grounds

This amendment has already been in force since 27 October 2026.

Amended Article 70 expressly refers to adverse weather such as typhoons, floods, heatwaves and cold waves. Before the amendment, broad references to events beyond the parties’ control or adverse weather left room for interpretation, but heatwaves and cold waves were not expressly listed.[7]

Adverse weather → delay to construction → need for an extension to prevent industrial accidents → contractor’s request → extension by the project owner absent special grounds. The mere existence of hot or cold weather does not automatically extend every project.

A related contractor may also request an extension from the construction contractor. Records should include weather warnings, work stoppages or reduced hours, schedule impacts and the safety basis for the extension.

11. Occupational safety and health management expenses expand across subcontracting and non-construction sectors

From 28 April 2027 — stronger construction-sector subcontracting rules

Amended Article 72(1) requires construction contractors to include occupational safety and health management expenses in the contract price when entering into subcontracts. Subcontractors also receive expanded duties concerning statements of use, record retention and prohibition on use for other purposes.[7]

From 28 October 2027 — designated non-construction subcontracting

New Article 72(5) requires contractors in industries to be prescribed by presidential decree to include occupational safety and health management expenses in subcontract prices. The former paragraph (5), dealing with use for other purposes, is moved to paragraph (6).[7]

The covered industries and detailed calculation standards remain to be specified. Manufacturing, shipbuilding, logistics, services and other businesses that rely heavily on subcontracting should check whether they fall within the final decree before commencement.[2]

12. A reward scheme for reporting OSH violations is introduced

From 28 January 2027, a person who reports a violation of the Act or an order under it may receive a reward within the available budget. The presidential decree will set eligibility, amounts and procedures; the Act currently establishes the legal basis only.[8]

Businesses should expect that concerns raised by workers, contractors and other site participants may more readily lead to external enforcement, in addition to ordinary inspections.

13. A Safe Workplace Committee will be established

From 28 January 2027, the Ministry of Employment and Labor will establish a Safe Workplace Committee chaired by the Minister. It will deliberate and coordinate major occupational safety and health policies, basic prevention plans, regulatory evaluation and improvement, cross-ministerial matters, and prevention research and development.[8]

The provision does not itself create a new workplace duty, but it creates a dedicated central policy body for future regulatory development.

14. LAW NOAH Analysis — revise the stop-work protocol before focusing on post-accident response

The reform is not only about the headline figure of five per cent. The legal framework increasingly controls who stops work at the risk-identification stage, what measures are taken and who approves resumption.

2) Keep a separate work-resumption approval record

Amended Articles 51, 52 and 53 reinforce the rule that work must not resume before the necessary measure is complete. The business should separately record the hazard, corrective action, confirmer, confirmation time, approver and resumption time.

If an accident later occurs, why the work was restarted may become a central issue.

3) Principal contractors should test actual site control, not only contract wording

  • Hazard reporting and stop-work procedure
  • Principal contractor–subcontractor contact structure
  • Emergency stop-work authority
  • Safety-measure costs and safety-management expenses
  • Work-resumption approval
  • Sharing of accident and hazard information
  • Handling direct requests from contractor workers
  • Prohibition on disadvantageous treatment

A clause stating that the subcontractor will comply with the Act is not enough. The arrangements must operate at the site.

4) Manage repeated accidents and enforcement history at enterprise level

The surcharge uses worker deaths within the most recent year, while the registration-cancellation request uses business suspensions during the most recent three years. A multi-site enterprise should centrally maintain the following chain:

Fatal accident → cause → statutory breach → corrective or suspension order → suspension request and disposition → recurrence-prevention measure → later accident

16. Matters not yet finalised or requiring further confirmation

As of 10 November 2026, the Act has been promulgated, but the following matters require subordinate legislation or future interpretation:

  • The meaning of “special grounds” under Article 51
  • The specific cases subject to the pre-completion work prohibition under Article 53(3)
  • The scope of serious physical or physiological impairment under Article 55
  • The detailed operating-profit calculation for the surcharge
  • The decree conditions for applying the KRW 3 billion ceiling where five per cent is below that amount
  • Adjustment rules and composition and operation of the surcharge review committee
  • Whether pre-commencement business suspensions count for the registration-cancellation request
  • Reward eligibility, amounts and procedure
  • Industries and calculation rules for non-construction safety-management expenses
  • Implementing rules and notices for the expanded stop-work framework

The Ministry has stated that it will prepare implementing legislation and enforcement systems. Businesses should therefore recheck the subordinate legislation before each commencement date.[2]

Conclusion

The amendments do more than increase penalties after a fatal accident.

They expand workers’ direct stop-work rights and requests to employers and principal contractors, restrict resumption before hazards are addressed, and strengthen economic and administrative consequences for repeated fatal accidents.

Hazard report or stop-work request → stop-work and evacuation → site assessment → corrective measure → completion confirmation → work-resumption approval and record → enterprise-level tracking of accidents and enforcement

The key is not merely to issue another policy document, but to determine—before commencement—who receives a risk report, who stops the work, who confirms safety and who approves resumption.
