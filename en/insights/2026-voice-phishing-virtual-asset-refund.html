South Korea’s 2026 Voice-Phishing Refund Reform — Virtual Assets Can Be Frozen and Recovered

From 1 October 2026, virtual-asset exchanges are covered by South Korea’s voice-phishing freeze-and-refund framework, including cases where stolen cash is converted into crypto.

Key points

  • The virtual-asset relief expansion has applied since 1 October 2026.
  • Virtual-asset exchanges are now part of the suspicious-transaction detection, temporary restriction, freezing and refund framework.
  • If stolen cash is converted into crypto, the refund analysis can follow the asset remaining when the freeze takes effect.
  • Crypto may be refunded by type and quantity, or sold for cash at the victim’s request.
  • Not every crypto scam falls within the Act; the conduct must qualify as statutory telecommunications financial fraud.
  • Speed remains critical before assets leave a regulated bank or exchange account for an external wallet.

01. Virtual-asset relief has applied since 1 October 2026

Act No. 21503, promulgated on 31 March 2026, took effect on 1 October 2026.[1]

The statute’s title was also changed from a law focused on refunding “victim funds” to one addressing “victim assets”, reflecting the expansion beyond money to virtual assets.

02. Why the law was amended

The earlier system was built mainly around bank-account freezes, extinguishment of claims and refunds.

Fraud increasingly involved victims being told to buy and transfer crypto, account takeovers, rapid cash-to-crypto conversion and movement through banks and exchanges. The reform connects those channels within one relief framework.[5]

03. “Victim assets” now include virtual assets

The amended Act includes not only money transferred from a victim’s bank account but also virtual assets transferred from the victim’s exchange account and other statutorily defined assets.[2]

This allows the procedure to address both direct crypto theft and cases that begin with cash but are converted into crypto during the fraud.

04. Voice phishing that tells victims to transfer crypto is covered

Statutory telecommunications financial fraud now expressly includes deceiving or threatening a person into transferring virtual assets, not only sending or remitting money.[2]

05. Not every crypto scam falls within this Act

The Act does not automatically cover every fraud involving crypto.

It generally excludes conduct disguised as the supply of goods or services. In Supreme Court case 2024Do6831, the Court explained that this exclusion ordinarily concerns situations where the supposed goods or services and the transferred economic benefit stand in a genuine exchange relationship.[4]

A transaction-like appearance without such a reciprocal exchange does not necessarily remove the case from the Act. The statute also expressly brings conduct disguised as providing, arranging or brokering a loan back within the scope of telecommunications financial fraud.

Ordinary investment losses, price declines after legitimate crypto trading and routine contractual disputes therefore should not be treated as automatic refund cases.

06. Virtual-asset exchanges are now part of the relief framework

The reform extends statutory duties beyond conventional financial institutions to virtual-asset exchanges.

Depending on the circumstances, exchanges must participate in suspicious-transaction detection, temporary account restrictions, identity checks, relief applications, freezes, information sharing and refunds.[5]

07. Exchanges can take temporary measures based on internal detection or external information

Article 2-5 addresses temporary measures where a financial institution or exchange identifies a suspected victim transaction through its own monitoring or receives relevant information under the Act.[2]

For an exchange, the measure may include delaying or temporarily stopping transfers from the relevant virtual-asset account while identity and other checks are performed.

08. Freezing now reaches both bank accounts and exchange accounts

The amended concept of a fraudulent account extends beyond bank accounts to relevant virtual-asset exchange accounts.

It can also reach follow-on accounts used to move money or virtual assets onward from earlier fraudulent accounts, subject to the statutory definition.

09. Victims can seek relief from exchanges

A victim may apply to the institution managing the account from which the victim asset was transferred or to the institution managing the fraudulent account.

Where the case involves virtual assets, the relevant exchange may therefore receive the application, and the framework allows follow-on freezing requests when assets move to another institution or exchange.

10. Urgent applications may be made first by phone or orally

Ordinarily, the victim files a written relief application with identification documents.

Where the circumstances are urgent or unavoidable, the victim may first apply by telephone or orally and must submit the formal documents within three business days.[3]

If the documents are not filed within that period, the institution may grant an additional 14-day submission period before treating the initial oral or telephone application as not having been made.

11. A formal freeze can follow the relief application

A freeze may follow a victim application, investigative request, statutory information sharing or other facts supporting suspicion that the account is being used for the fraud.

For virtual assets, the record includes the asset type, quantity, price at the time of freezing and converted amount.

12. What remains at the moment of freezing matters

If the asset lost by the victim differs from the asset remaining in the frozen account, the Enforcement Decree treats the asset existing in the account at the time of the freeze as the refund asset.[3]

Cash stolen from a victim may therefore be refunded as crypto if the fraudster converted it before the freeze; conversely, stolen crypto may be refunded as cash if it had already been sold and cash remained.

13. Virtual assets are valued using the price at the time of freezing

Where different assets must be compared for proportional allocation, the Enforcement Decree uses a structure based on asset quantity multiplied by the price at the time of freezing.[3]

14. Crypto refunds are determined by asset type and quantity

Where the refund asset itself is virtual currency, the system determines it by asset type and quantity, rather than fixing a single won amount.

15. Victims may request sale and payment in cash

A victim receiving a virtual-asset refund may ask for the asset to be sold and the proceeds paid in cash. The exchange may sell the refund asset and transfer the cash proceeds to the victim.[6]

16. Full recovery is not guaranteed

The system does not make the state or exchange an insurer of the full loss.

If little remains when the freeze occurs, recovery is correspondingly limited. Where several victims share insufficient remaining assets, the statutory proportional-allocation rules apply.

17. Mixed cash and crypto losses can be allocated proportionally

The Enforcement Decree allows cash losses and virtual assets valued at the freeze-time price to be compared when calculating each victim’s share of the total loss.

18. The claim-extinguishment procedure follows the freeze

After freezing, the relevant institution generally asks the Financial Supervisory Service to commence the claim-extinguishment public-notice procedure.

For virtual assets, the notice may include the asset type and quantity. Ordinarily, two months after the initial notice, the relevant claim is extinguished within the announced scope and the refund determination follows.

19. Additional victims may apply within two months after public notice

A victim who did not apply before the claim-extinguishment notice may generally submit an additional relief application within two months after the notice.

This is not a reason to delay the first report because the assets may leave the regulated account before any freeze occurs.

20. The Financial Supervisory Service determines the recipient and refund asset

After the relevant claim is extinguished, the Financial Supervisory Service generally determines the person entitled to receive the refund and the amount, type or quantity within 14 days and notifies the parties.

21. Full prior compensation or participation in the crime can bar payment

A victim who has already received full compensation, a person who obtained improper benefit from the fraud, or a participant in the crime may be excluded from payment under the statutory rules.

22. False victim applications may create civil and criminal liability

A person who falsely seeks relief and causes loss to the account holder may face civil damages liability.

Article 16 also punishes a false relief application by up to three years’ imprisonment or a KRW 30 million criminal fine.[2]

23. Account holders may object and litigate

The holder of a frozen bank or exchange account has procedural rights as well.

The holder may object on statutory grounds, including by presenting objective evidence that the account is not a fraudulent account or that the asset was acquired under a legitimate legal basis. Related civil litigation can also affect the commencement or progress of the claim-extinguishment procedure.

24. Pre-1 October 2026 virtual-asset losses may also require review

Article 2 of the supplementary provisions to Act No. 21503 applies the amended definition in Article 2(2) and the victim-relief application rules in Article 3 to qualifying virtual-asset fraud occurring before commencement and its victims.[1]

Accordingly, a pre-1 October 2026 loss should not be rejected solely because of its date. Actual recovery, however, may be impossible if no asset remains in an account that can still be frozen.

25. Crypto already moved to a private wallet presents practical limits

The amendment does not give an exchange power to recover every asset anywhere on a blockchain.

If the assets have already left regulated bank or exchange accounts for an external private wallet and no freezeable asset remains, the Act’s rapid refund procedure has practical limits.

Blockchain tracing, criminal investigation and confiscation or collection of criminal proceeds may still arise under separate criminal-law procedures.

26. What victims should do immediately

Victims should preserve bank details, exchange information, the recipient’s account information, asset type and quantity, wallet addresses, transaction hashes, timestamps, messages, call records, phone numbers, IDs, URLs and exchange deposit/withdrawal records.

They should notify the relevant bank, exchange and investigative authorities promptly and ask whether a freeze or transfer restriction is still possible.

28. Exchanges also need stronger internal controls

Exchanges need operational controls for suspicious-transaction detection, temporary restrictions, identity verification, formal freezes, inter-institution information sharing, refunds and record keeping.

Because virtual assets can move to other accounts or external wallets rapidly, delayed detection can sharply reduce recovery prospects.

29. LAW NOAH Analysis — the reform closes the bank-to-crypto gap

The reform matters because it follows where the criminal proceeds move, rather than focusing only on the form of the victim’s original loss.

It connects bank account → virtual-asset exchange → exchange account within one relief framework and permits the refund asset to reflect what remains when the freeze occurs.

But the reform does not automatically recover crypto that has already escaped to an external wallet. Speed remains decisive: victims should notify the bank, exchange and investigative authorities while the asset is still in a controllable account.
