South Korea’s 2027 Game Industry Act Reform — Stronger Domestic-Agent Rules and New User-Complaint Duties

From 21 April 2027, user-harm and complaint handling becomes a statutory duty for game-related businesses, while qualifying foreign game companies face a materially stronger domestic-agent regime.

Key points

  • The amendment takes effect on 21 April 2027.
  • Game-related business operators must address harm to domestic users and user complaints.
  • Domestic agents of qualifying foreign game companies will also handle user complaints and regulatory document-production requests.
  • Where specified Korean affiliates exist, the domestic agent must be selected from that affiliate pool, and headquarters must educate and supervise the agent.
  • Existing agents may need to be redesignated within six months after commencement.
  • Domestic-agent violations can lead to fines, corrective orders and, after noncompliance, network-level restrictions on handling the game.

01. The reform takes effect on 21 April 2027

Act No. 22085 was promulgated on 20 October 2026 and takes effect on 21 April 2027.[1]

The reform adds user-complaint duties, strengthens the domestic-agent regime for qualifying foreign game companies, creates new document-production powers for regulators and links domestic-agent violations to fines, corrective orders and network-level restrictions.

02. The domestic-agent regime already exists

South Korea’s foreign-game domestic-agent regime was introduced by a 2024 amendment and has been in force since 23 October 2025.

Qualifying foreign distributors and game-service providers without a Korean address or place of business already have to appoint a domestic agent for specified statutory functions. The 2027 reform expands that existing framework rather than creating it from scratch.

03. User-harm and complaint handling becomes a statutory duty

New Article 28(1)7-2 requires game-related business operators to address harm to domestic users and user complaints.[2]

The significance is that user-protection and complaint response becomes the operator’s own statutory compliance duty, not merely an area in which businesses cooperate with government-led protection measures.

04. ‘Game-related business operator’ is broader than foreign publishers

The Game Industry Act uses a broader category covering businesses engaged in game production, distribution, provision and other regulated game-related activities.

Accordingly, the new Article 28 duty is not limited to foreign companies. The domestic-agent obligation under Article 31-2 is a separate rule targeted at specified foreign distributors and service providers without a Korean address or place of business.

05. E-commerce law governs complaint handling for mail-order intermediaries

Article 28(1)7-2 expressly directs matters concerning mail-order intermediaries to Article 20(3) of South Korea’s E-Commerce Act.[4]

The Enforcement Decree requires complaint-handling personnel and facilities, pre-established dispute standards, notice of progress within three business days and notice of an investigation result or proposed resolution within ten business days.[5]

A game company may simultaneously be a game-related business operator and a mail-order intermediary, so both statutory frameworks may matter. The specific complaint-handling pathway for intermediation, however, is routed through the E-Commerce Act.

06. The new duty does not guarantee every refund or damages claim

The amendment does not automatically require a refund, item restoration, account reinstatement or damages payment whenever a user complains.

It requires a compliant system for addressing user harm and complaints. The legal outcome of a particular claim still depends on the contract, terms of use, consumer law, civil law and the facts.

07. Not every foreign game company must appoint a domestic agent

The obligation applies to a foreign game distributor or service provider that lacks a Korean address or place of business and meets thresholds prescribed by Presidential Decree.

08. The current Decree uses three designation triggers

As of November 2026, Article 18-3 of the Enforcement Decree identifies three triggers:[3]

TriggerCurrent rule
RevenuePrior-year total revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion
Korean mobile installationsDistribution or provision of a game averaging at least 1,000 daily installations on mobile devices sold in Korea in the prior year
Regulatory needAn event or risk materially disrupting distribution order that leads to a reporting demand by the Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism

The Decree uses “total revenue” and converts foreign-currency revenue using the prior year’s average exchange rate. These are the current thresholds and should be rechecked before the 2027 commencement date.

09. Domestic-agent functions expand materially in 2027

The amended Article 31-2 adds domestic user-harm and complaint handling and responses to new Article 39-3 document-production requests to the domestic agent’s functions, in addition to existing reporting and game-labelling tasks.

10. Certain Korean affiliates must be used as domestic agents

Where specified Korean entities exist, the domestic agent must be selected from them. The amended statute identifies:

  1. a Korean corporation established by the foreign game business;
  2. a Korean corporation over which the foreign game business exercises dominant influence over board composition or business operations, as further prescribed by Presidential Decree; and
  3. a corporation capable of faithfully performing the domestic-agent functions.

Category 3 is statutory wording, but it may be used only where no Category 1 or 2 entity exists.

11. Categories 1 and 2 are a priority pool, not a sequential ladder

The statute is better read as requiring selection from the Category 1 or Category 2 affiliate pool whenever any such entity exists. Only where neither exists may a Category 3 corporation be used.

12. ‘Dominant influence’ will be detailed by Presidential Decree

The statute refers to dominant influence over matters such as officer composition or business operations, but the detailed test remains delegated.

Shareholding, voting rights, appointment powers and operational control may be relevant, but the final implementing Decree must be checked before making a compliance determination.

13. Existing domestic agents may have to be replaced within six months

The supplementary provisions require a foreign game business that already has a domestic agent at commencement and also has a qualifying Korean affiliate under the amended Article 31-2(2) to redesignate its domestic agent from that affiliate pool within six months after commencement.[1]

On the ordinary date calculation, that means by 21 October 2027. Companies using outside agents should review termination rights, transition of files and complaints, and changes to terms-of-use contact information.

14. The domestic agent itself receives statutory compliance duties

Amended Article 31-2(3) directly requires the domestic agent to comply with the functions assigned under paragraph (1).

The agent therefore needs real staff, authority, access to information and escalation channels for user complaints, regulatory reports, game labelling and document-production requests.

15. The foreign head office must supervise the domestic agent

Amended Article 31-2(4) requires the appointing game business to educate the domestic agent and supervise it, including by checking the status of its work.

Appointment therefore does not shift all responsibility away from headquarters.

16. Detailed education and supervision methods remain delegated

The frequency and content of education, inspection methods, recordkeeping and related details remain matters for the implementing Decree.

The underlying duty to educate and supervise, however, is already fixed by statute.

17. Domestic-agent designation must continue throughout Korean service

Amended Article 31-2(5) requires uninterrupted designation while the game is distributed or made available for use in Korea and restricts arbitrary changes outside the prescribed procedure.

18. Terms must disclose domestic-agent contact information

Terms of use must continue to state the domestic agent’s name or corporate name and representative, address or place of business, telephone number and email address.

19. Domestic-agent violations are attributed to the foreign game business

Where a domestic agent violates the Act in connection with its delegated statutory functions, the Act treats the game-related business operator as having committed that act.

This attribution rule becomes more important as the delegated functions expand to complaints and document production, although each specific penalty or corrective order still has its own legal elements.

20. The domestic agent must maintain effective contact with headquarters

The domestic agent must maintain an effective means of communication with the foreign game business.

A nominal postal address is not enough for practical compliance. The agent needs to escalate complaints, obtain decisions and records, respond to regulators and update reporting or disclosure information.

21. MCST and GRAC receive a new document-production power

New Article 39-3 allows the Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism or the Game Rating and Administration Committee to require necessary goods, documents and other materials where:

  1. a statutory violation is found or suspected;
  2. a report or complaint concerning a violation is received;
  3. information is needed to determine whether the domestic-agent threshold is met; or
  4. another Decree-prescribed verification need exists.

22. Regulators may demand data to verify designation thresholds

A foreign game company’s assertion that it falls below the domestic-agent thresholds is not conclusive. Regulators may request revenue, installation and service data to verify the threshold analysis.

23. Non-production or false production may trigger fines up to KRW 10 million

Failure, without justifiable reason, to comply with an Article 39-3 request—or submitting false materials—may result in an administrative fine of up to KRW 10 million.

Detailed fine schedules should be rechecked in the final implementing Decree.

24. Three domestic-agent violations may trigger fines up to KRW 20 million

Amended Article 48(1) expressly covers three categories with an administrative fine of up to KRW 20 million:

  • failure to appoint a required domestic agent;
  • failure to designate the agent from the statutorily required Korean-affiliate pool; and
  • failure by headquarters to educate, manage and supervise the domestic agent.

The continuing-designation and change-control duty in Article 31-2(5) is not separately listed in Article 48(1), although a violation of Article 31-2 can still enter the corrective-order framework.

25. Domestic-agent violations can lead to corrective recommendations and orders

Amended Article 38(9) creates a corrective framework for violations of Article 31-2.

A person receiving a corrective recommendation or order must complete the required measures and report the result under the statutory procedure, with the amendment specifying a seven-day implementation period.

26. Noncompliance can lead to network-level handling restrictions

The amended Act directly links the following steps:

Article 31-2 violation → Article 38(9) corrective order → noncompliance → Article 38(7)2 network-level handling restriction.

The Minister may order an information-communications service provider or bulletin-board operator to refuse, suspend or restrict handling of the relevant game.

This is legally different from directly imposing a business-suspension order on the foreign company, but it can materially affect distribution, access and use of the game in Korea.

27. What changes for users

For games subject to the domestic-agent regime, Korean users should have a stronger domestic channel for user harm, payment and refund complaints, account issues, operational complaints and statutory disclosure issues.

Where a qualifying Korean affiliate exists, the reform is designed to connect the domestic response function more closely to the foreign headquarters. It does not, however, create an automatic right to refund, item restoration or damages.

28. What foreign game companies should check now

  • Whether the company currently meets a domestic-agent threshold.
  • How current revenue and mobile-installation thresholds are calculated.
  • Whether the company has a directly established Korean corporation.
  • Whether it exercises dominant influence over another Korean entity.
  • Whether the current outside domestic agent can remain after the reform.
  • Whether the six-month redesignation rule applies.
  • Termination, amendment and handover provisions in the existing agent contract.
  • Escalation channels for user harm and complaints.
  • The agent’s authority and access to records for reports, labels and document production.
  • Records of training and work-status reviews.
  • Continuous designation throughout Korean service.
  • Current domestic-agent details in terms of use.
  • Readiness to provide revenue, installation and operational data to regulators.
  • Ongoing monitoring of the 2027 implementing Decree.

29. Korean game companies should also revisit complaint-handling systems

The domestic-agent amendments principally target qualifying foreign businesses, but the new Article 28 user-protection duty is broader.

Korean game-related businesses should review customer-service operations, complaint records, payment and refund workflows, account-suspension appeals, operational escalation, recurring-complaint analysis and consumer-dispute playbooks.

Businesses that also act as mail-order intermediaries should separately implement the Article 20(3) E-Commerce Act process. The existence of that complaint-handling duty alone does not automatically create joint liability for all seller-related loss; the E-Commerce Act’s separate liability provisions have their own conditions.

30. Several matters remain for the implementing Decree

Act No. 22085 itself is final as of 16 November 2026.

Detailed standards for dominant influence, education and supervision, maintaining or changing the agent, document-production procedures and fine schedules still require implementing rules. The current threshold criteria may also change before April 2027.

Companies should therefore separate obligations already fixed by statute from details that still depend on the final Decree.

31. LAW NOAH Analysis — the point is a real accountable Korean node, not merely a Korean contact

The existing domestic-agent regime primarily ensured a Korean contact point for foreign game businesses.

The 2027 amendment goes further: it adds user-harm and complaint handling and document-production responses, imposes head-office training and supervision, and prioritises qualifying Korean affiliates as the accountable domestic agent.

It then connects document production → corrective recommendation/order → network-level refusal, suspension or restriction after noncompliance.

Foreign game companies therefore need an operating chain that actually works: head office → Korean affiliate/domestic agent → customer support → legal/compliance → GRAC/MCST.

The core of the reform is not another Korean mailing address. It is a domestic structure capable of taking responsibility for users and regulators in practice.
