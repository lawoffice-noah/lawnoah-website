South Korea’s Lodging Platform Rules from 12 November 2026 — Verification of Lodging-Business Notifications and Administrative Fines

From 12 November 2026, lodging platforms must verify the notification status of lodging businesses covered by the Public Sanitation Control Act, and brokering unverified listings is restricted. This report separates the direct statutory duty from the practical verification of accommodation governed by other statutes.

Key points

  • The rules take effect on 12 November 2026.
  • The direct statutory verification duty applies to intermediation of lodging businesses governed by the Public Sanitation Control Act.
  • Platforms must verify the lodging operator’s business-notification certificate.
  • If the certificate cannot be verified, brokering the listing is generally prohibited.
  • Where an unreported lodging operator is discovered in the platform’s cybermall and the listing is immediately deleted, the statute provides an exception to the brokering prohibition.
  • Listings already on the platform before commencement also require review if they remain available after commencement.
  • The Act provides a ceiling of KRW 10 million; the Enforcement Decree sets the prescribed amount at KRW 5 million.
  • Rural homestays, foreign-tourist urban homestays and hanok-experience businesses are excluded from the Act’s lodging-business category and must be classified separately.

01. Why the law changed

Operating a lodging business without the required notification was already unlawful. What was missing was a Public Sanitation Control Act duty requiring an online lodging intermediary to verify whether the accommodation it brokered had completed the statutory lodging-business notification.

The amendment therefore moves beyond enforcement against operators alone and introduces a control at the online distribution and intermediation stage.[2]

02. Article 4-2 does not cover every type of accommodation

New Article 4-2 applies where an e-commerce intermediary brokers sales at the request of a person conducting a lodging business under the Public Sanitation Control Act.[1]

The Enforcement Decree excludes, among others, rural homestay facilities, accommodation in natural recreation forests, specified youth-training facilities, registered foreign-tourist urban homestays and registered hanok-experience facilities from the Act’s lodging-business category.[4]

It would therefore be inaccurate to describe Article 4-2 as directly requiring a Public Sanitation Control Act notification certificate for every lawful accommodation listing. Verification of the separate statutory basis for excluded accommodation is better understood as a practical classification and compliance step, rather than the same Article 4-2 duty. The Ministry of Health and Welfare has nevertheless instructed platforms to review existing listings for a notification certificate or other basis showing lawful accommodation operations.[3]

03. Hotels and condominium resorts require separate classification

Unlike foreign-tourist urban homestays and hanok-experience businesses, tourism accommodation such as hotels and condominium resorts is not excluded from the Public Sanitation Control Act’s lodging-business category.

Article 18 of the Tourism Promotion Act provides a deemed-notification mechanism: once the relevant tourism business is duly registered, the operator may be deemed to have filed the lodging-business notification required under Article 3 of the Public Sanitation Control Act.[5] Administrative interpretation also recognises that the deemed notification is linked to the continuing validity of the tourism-business registration.[6]

04. A business registration certificate is not enough

A tax/business registration and a lodging-business notification under the Public Sanitation Control Act are separate legal processes. The existence of a business registration number does not establish that the lodging-business notification has been completed.

The Supreme Court has described the lodging-business notification as an administrative notification that is subject to acceptance once statutory facility and equipment requirements are satisfied. It is therefore legally distinct from ordinary tax registration.[7]

A platform should manage the process as classify the accommodation → identify the governing statute → verify the required notification or registration → match the evidence to the actual listing.

05. Unverified lodging businesses generally may not be brokered

Article 4-2(2) prohibits e-commerce intermediation where the required lodging-business notification certificate cannot be verified. Continuing to make an unverified Public Sanitation Control Act lodging listing reservable after 12 November 2026 may therefore expose the intermediary to an administrative fine.[1]

The Ministry has also stated that listings whose lawful operating status has not been verified should not continue to be brokered after commencement.[3]

06. Immediate deletion operates as a statutory exception

Article 4-2(2) prohibits brokering where the certificate cannot be verified, but expressly excludes a case where the platform discovers an operator conducting lodging business without notification in its cybermall and immediately deletes the listing.[1]

The more accurate description is therefore not that a separate deletion provision independently creates a general deletion duty, but that immediate deletion is built into the statutory exception to the brokering prohibition.

The exception should not be treated as a rule that retrospectively cures an earlier period of continued unlawful intermediation. Platforms should record the time of discovery → reservation or display block → completed deletion.

07. Indirect OTA supply may still fall within the rules

A platform is not automatically outside the regime merely because it did not contract directly with the lodging operator. The Ministry’s official guidance states that platforms that receive lodging inventory from another OTA or supplier, link or display it on their own interface and actually intermediate the sale are also within the intended scope.[3]

Whether a particular service is an e-commerce intermediary depends on the real transaction structure: who receives the consumer’s order, who processes reservation and payment, whose transaction is being connected, and whether the sale is actually facilitated on the platform’s interface.

08. Existing listings also require review

The Ministry has expressly stated that listings registered before commencement must also be reviewed if they will continue to be brokered after 12 November 2026.[3]

09. The prescribed fine is KRW 5 million

The amended Act allows an administrative fine of up to KRW 10 million for intermediation in violation of Article 4-2(2). The amended Enforcement Decree sets the specific prescribed amount for this violation at KRW 5 million.[2]

Does every unreported listing generate a separate KRW 5 million fine?

The Act and Decree do not, by themselves, justify a categorical formula such as “10 listings = KRW 5 million × 10”. The unit of violation may depend on the identity and continuity of the conduct and the specific facts.

Voluntary-payment reduction

The Act on the Regulation of Violations of Public Order applies to administrative fines. Where a person voluntarily pays during the opinion-submission period after prior notice, a reduction of up to 20% may be available under that Act and its Enforcement Decree.[8]

10. Criminal liability of unreported lodging operators remains

The amendment adds duties for intermediaries; it does not replace the operator’s existing duty to notify. A person operating a lodging business without the required notification may face imprisonment for up to two years or a criminal fine of up to KRW 20 million.

11. LAW NOAH Analysis — classify the legal type before collecting documents

The weakest response would be to require every listing to upload the same Public Sanitation Control Act certificate. Different accommodation types have different legal bases.

1) Classify each listing first

General/living accommodation / tourism accommodation / rural homestay / foreign-tourist urban homestay / hanok-experience business / other.

2) Link the correct legal basis

  • General and living accommodation → Public Sanitation Control Act notification
  • Tourism accommodation → Tourism Promotion Act registration and any deemed-notification effect
  • Rural homestay → Rural Community Improvement Act notification
  • Foreign-tourist urban homestay / hanok experience → Tourism Promotion Act registration

The direct Article 4-2 notification-verification duty should not be conflated with the platform’s practical verification of legal status under other statutes.

3) Match the evidence to the live listing

Operator → property → address → business type → notification/registration → platform listing should correspond. The Supreme Court has also emphasised that the lodging-notification system must enable the competent authority to identify the actual business operator clearly.[7]

4) Record the response after discovery

Time discovered → internal verification → reservation block → deletion → operator notice → evidence retention. Because Article 4-2 connects immediate deletion to a statutory exception, timing and documentation may matter in an enforcement proceeding.

Conclusion

From 12 November 2026, lodging platforms can no longer treat Public Sanitation Control Act lodging listings as ordinary inventory requiring no legal-status verification. At the same time, the reform should not be reduced to “collect one certificate from every accommodation provider”.

Classify the accommodation → verify the Public Sanitation Control Act notification where applicable → verify the separate legal basis for other lawful accommodation → intermediate only verified listings → immediately delete an unreported lodging listing when discovered → retain the verification and deletion record.

The amendment does not transfer all accommodation enforcement to platforms. It creates a new control at the point where online intermediaries previously could continue distributing unreported lodging inventory without a specific Public Sanitation Control Act verification duty.
